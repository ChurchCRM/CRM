# ChurchCRM 7.8.8 Release Notes

**Release Date**: November 25, 2026
**Theme**: A calmer, clearer start — for churches trying ChurchCRM and the ones already running it

This release follows the journeys a church takes with ChurchCRM: trying it, installing it, upgrading it, and using it every week. New installs open on a guided Setup Dashboard, the demo is smoother, upgrades go ahead on older databases with a clear warning that the database is a security risk, and weekly users get one calm place for every notice and counts that reflect their active congregation.

---

## 👋 New installs and the demo

* **A calmer first start.** A fresh install opens on the Setup Dashboard, with three clear paths: **explore with the demo data** — a full sample church across Finance, Events, Sunday School, and the Member Portal — **set up your church**, with a Next Steps checklist that walks the first steps in order, or **restore a backup**. The dashboard speaks your browser's language from the first visit.
* **Demo mode lives in the bell.** The demo notice sits in the notification center in the top bar, alongside everything else that needs an admin's attention — and turning the demo into your real church is one click away, from the notification center or the Setup Dashboard.
* **The database step explains itself.** The setup wizard's database step uses plain language — "Database server address," not server jargon — highlights the exact field that needs attention when a connection fails, and links to the database setup guide. If ChurchCRM can't start at all, the error page names the exact problem.
* **Installs go ahead on older databases.** Churches on shared hosting get a clear path: if your host's database no longer receives security fixes (MariaDB older than 10.11, MySQL older than 8.4), the install proceeds, and the warning waits for you in the notification center.

## 🛡️ Upgrading

* **Upgrades go ahead.** Churches on shared hosting can move forward at last: an out-of-support database now comes with a clear warning — a database that no longer receives security fixes is a security risk — and the upgrade proceeds. The warning sits in the notification center with a red badge, on every page until an admin dismisses it.
* **Fresh look after upgrading:** page styling refreshes itself on upgrade, so every new screen arrives looking right.
* **Settings live with their work now.** The old all-in-one System Settings page gives way to the Settings hub (Admin → Settings): Quick Search, Sign-in & Security, Text Messages, Public Calendar, Scheduled Tasks, and Reporting each have their own section, report settings sit inside the People and Finance hubs beside the reports they shape, and every settings panel saves itself as you go — no save button to hunt for.

## 🔔 Every week: one calm center, clearer numbers

### For administrators
* **Banners become a bell.** Demo mode, the database warning, two-factor sign-in reminders, update notices, and announcements all live behind one notification icon in the top bar — there when you want them, out of the way when you don't. Announcements appear once per login on the landing page, then wait in the center.
* **Finance Settings is a front door.** It opens as a single hub linking to dedicated editors — Pledge Reminders, Zero Givers, Deposits & Payments — and the QuickBooks deposit-ticket layout is now 18 labeled fields in millimetres, measured the way the printed form is.
* **Simpler privacy choice.** Sharing is now one clear choice — whether to share which pages are used — and the question waits for you in the notification center, ready when you are. Problem reports flow either way, so bugs get fixed faster; if you had sharing off before, those reports now flow too. No church information is ever included.

### For people, groups, and check-in
* **Active means active — everywhere.** Member classifications you mark inactive take effect on every output: CSV exports, cart emails, mailing labels, name badges, group directories, photo books, and pledge reminders all reflect your active classifications.
* **Dashboards count the congregation you have.** Deactivating one person updates the People dashboard totals and the classification, gender, family-role, and age charts right away — while their household stays in your active Families count. Self-registrations join the counts, labels, and exports once they're approved.
* **Groups at creation:** add a new person and place them in their groups right there, in the same step.
* **Convert to Family, right on the person page:** an individual record becomes a family from its own Actions menu.
* **Calmer check-in:** checked-in people stay right where they are on the roster, highlighted, with Undo and Check Out at hand — and the attendance counts update live, no page reload. Kiosk check-in runs more reliably on shared hosting, too.

### Small fixes
* **Wide tables:** action menus in long, sideways-scrolling lists stay attached to their row — on phones, the family member menu sits neatly on its button again.
* **Finance dashboard:** the summary pills link to the right page.
* **Setup Dashboard:** once your demo data is loaded, the demo card steps aside.
* **Trying the demo:** exploring with the demo data carries no Google Analytics tracking.
